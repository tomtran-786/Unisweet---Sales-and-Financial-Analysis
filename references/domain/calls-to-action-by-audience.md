# Calls to Action by Audience

## Management message

> **Recover OLIVE-MT at customer x SKU level, reset trade-spend effectiveness, and selectively reinvest protected profit only where initiatives demonstrate incremental Gross Profit and PBO.**

The actions are designed around one tension: FY2024 PBO increased, but GSV, TO, Gross Profit and market share all declined. Management should protect the profit outcome without treating lower marketing expenditure as a sustainable growth strategy.

## Evidence that requires action

| Finding | Data-backed evidence | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| Topline weakened | GSV -6.0%; TO -6.7% | The issue is broader than discount alone |
| Discount intensity increased | Discount/GSV +62 bps; arithmetic TO counterfactual approximately EUR2.17m | Test trade-spend incrementality rather than applying broad discounts |
| Share loss is concentrated | UniSweet -1.97 points; OLIVE -2.98 points | Restore OLIVE while preserving SKY and COBALT momentum |
| MT is the relative hotspot | UniSweet MT share -6.07 points; OLIVE-MT share -7.88 points | Prioritise MT turnaround, while retaining a DT recovery branch for the larger absolute TO gap |
| Customer and SKU losses are concentrated | Bliss, Candies and Macarons represent 91% of the TO decline; POUCH 900GR and PACK 1.1KG are the two largest product gaps | Manage recovery at customer x SKU grain |
| PBO protection is cost-led | Gross Profit -EUR10.3m; Marketing Expense -EUR15.5m; PBO +EUR5.2m | Reinvest selectively with financial gates, not through a blanket budget restoration |

The data does **not** demonstrate meaningful customer attrition, a holiday cause, promotion causality or improved supply-chain efficiency. Those interpretations require additional operational evidence.

## Action 1 - Launch an OLIVE Customer x SKU Recovery Programme

### Objective

Recover profitable TO at the points of highest value loss, with OLIVE-MT as the strategic priority and a parallel DT root-cause workstream for Candies.

### Evidence and priority matrix

- OLIVE generated -EUR16.63m, or 85% of the total company TO decline.
- OLIVE-MT TO declined 14.9%, while OLIVE-MT market value declined 16.7% and share fell 7.88 points.
- Bliss-MT and Macarons-MT declined by EUR6.89m and EUR4.35m respectively.
- Bliss x OLIVE x POUCH 900GR declined EUR5.65m.
- Macarons x OLIVE x POUCH 900GR declined EUR2.66m.
- Candies-DT declined EUR6.47m; Candies x OLIVE x PACK 1.1KG declined EUR3.21m.

The first recovery matrix should therefore cover:

```text
MT customers: Bliss and Macarons
DT diagnostic branch: Candies
Priority products: POUCH 900GR and PACK 1.1KG
Secondary declining product: POUCH 100GR
```

### Accountable owners

- **Accountable:** Sales Head
- **Responsible:** MT Key Account Lead, DT Key Account Lead and OLIVE Brand/Category Lead
- **Financial challenge and validation:** Finance Business Partner
- **Operational support:** Customer Service, Supply Planning and Revenue Growth Management

### First 12 weeks

| Timing | Work | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 0-2 | Reconcile listings, assortment, price, promotion, sell-in, POS, inventory and OOS by priority customer x SKU | Root-cause tree and clean baseline |
| Weeks 3-4 | Agree one or two interventions per customer x SKU | Test design, economics and owner |
| Weeks 5-8 | Run controlled customer/SKU recovery tests | Weekly execution and financial scorecard |
| Weeks 9-12 | Scale, redesign or stop each intervention | Documented decision and next funding tranche |

### Intervention menu

- Restore listing, shelf availability or replenishment where availability is validated as the constraint.
- Correct assortment and pack architecture by account; do not assume one national pack solution.
- Use customer-specific price and promotion tests only where a clean baseline and incrementality measurement exist.
- Protect growing formats such as POUCH 400GR, BAR 50GR and BOTTLE 1KG from unintended cannibalisation.
- Diagnose Candies-DT separately because DT is not the same competitive or execution problem as MT.

### Core KPIs

- GSV and TO versus same-period prior year and agreed baseline
- Incremental TO, Gross Profit and PBO
- Discount/GSV and Discount/TO
- Weighted/numeric distribution, listings and assortment compliance
- In-stock rate, OOS, fill rate and OTIF
- Customer x SKU contribution and cannibalisation

### Decision gates

1. Do not fund an intervention until the customer x SKU baseline and expected economics are documented.
2. Continue a test only if execution compliance is measurable; otherwise fix execution before judging commercial effectiveness.
3. Scale only when incremental Gross Profit is positive and the PBO/payback guardrail is met.
4. Stop or redesign actions that add sell-in without evidence of POS movement or sustainable inventory.

### Additional data required

- Weekly POS units/value and customer inventory
- Promotion calendar, mechanic and display compliance
- List price, invoice price, rebates and total trade spend
- Distribution/listing history and shelf availability
- OOS, fill rate, OTIF and returns
- Unit/product cost and customer cost-to-serve

## Action 2 - Reset Trade-Spend and Discount ROI

### Objective

Replace broad discount decisions with customer x SKU investment rules based on incremental sales and margin.

### Evidence

- Total Discount/GSV increased 62 bps while TO declined 6.7%.
- Holding the FY2023 Discount/GSV rate on FY2024 GSV gives an arithmetic TO counterfactual of approximately EUR2.17m.
- POUCH 900GR declined 12.8% while Discount/GSV increased 103 bps.
- Bliss declined 36.0% while Discount/GSV increased 99 bps.
- Candies declined 15.2% while Discount/GSV increased 45 bps.
- Macarons declined 25.7% even though Discount/GSV improved 83 bps, confirming that discount alone is neither the complete cause nor the complete solution.

The EUR2.17m figure is a rate-hold counterfactual, not a measured promotion saving or a guaranteed recovery target.

### Accountable owners

- **Accountable:** Sales Head
- **Commercial design:** Revenue Growth Management and Key Account Leads
- **ROI validation and guardrails:** Finance Business Partner
- **Brand strategy:** OLIVE Brand/Category Lead

### Timing

| Timing | Work | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 0-2 | Build the trade-spend waterfall and promotion calendar by customer x SKU | Reconciled spend baseline |
| Weeks 3-4 | Segment promotions into keep, test, redesign and stop | Decision register and test backlog |
| Weeks 5-12 | Run controlled mechanics with pre-agreed financial thresholds | Incrementality and ROI results |
| Monthly thereafter | Reallocate spend to the best-performing interventions | Updated customer investment plan |

### Commercial rules

- Do not judge promotions on sell-in uplift alone.
- Separate base sales, forward buying, cannibalisation and post-promotion dip where data permits.
- Define customer- and SKU-specific discount ceilings rather than one portfolio-wide ceiling.
- Treat new/small-base formats separately from established formats so extreme growth rates do not distort decisions.
- Use Macarons as evidence that reducing discount is not sufficient when assortment, demand or execution is the binding constraint.

### Core KPIs

- Incremental GSV and TO versus baseline/control
- Incremental Gross Profit and PBO
- Promotion ROI and payback
- Discount/GSV and Discount/TO
- Uplift, post-promotion dip and cannibalisation
- Customer contribution after trade spend and cost-to-serve

### Decision gates

1. **Keep:** proven positive incremental Gross Profit and compliant execution.
2. **Test:** plausible commercial hypothesis but insufficient evidence.
3. **Redesign:** sales response exists but margin, payback or execution misses the guardrail.
4. **Stop:** no measurable incrementality or structurally negative incremental contribution.
5. Exceptions above the discount ceiling require joint Sales Head and Finance Head approval.

### Additional data required

- Promotion-level spend and mechanics
- Regular and promoted price
- Weekly POS volume/value and baseline methodology
- Product cost, customer funding and cost-to-serve
- Display, distribution and execution compliance
- Inventory before, during and after the promotion

## Action 3 - Selectively Reinvest Protected Profit with Portfolio Guardrails

### Objective

Use a controlled portion of the marketing savings to fund measurable recovery and scalable growth without giving back the FY2024 PBO protection.

### Evidence

- Gross Profit declined EUR10.3m.
- Marketing Expense declined EUR15.5m.
- PBO increased EUR5.2m and PBO Margin increased 393 bps.
- Supply Chain Cost declined in absolute terms, but Supply Chain Cost/TO worsened by approximately 35 bps; this is not evidence of improved efficiency.
- COBALT internal TO grew 11.6%, market value grew 3.9% and value share gained 0.56 points.
- SKY internal TO declined 7.4%, while market value grew 23.8% and share gained 0.45 points. The divergence must be diagnosed before investment is scaled.

### Accountable owners

- **Accountable:** Finance Head
- **Portfolio recommendation:** Finance Business Partner, Marketing Head and Sales Head
- **Initiative ownership:** Relevant Brand/Category Lead and Channel Lead
- **Service and feasibility challenge:** Supply Chain Lead

### Funding approach

Create three explicitly funded portfolios:

1. **OLIVE Recovery:** customer x SKU tests from Action 1.
2. **COBALT Scale:** expand proven customer/channel opportunities while monitoring the higher investment rate and PBO margin.
3. **SKY Diagnostic:** reconcile market measurement, sell-in, inventory and distribution before committing scale funding.

Do not restore the full marketing reduction as a single budget action. Release funding in tranches against measured results.

### Timing and stage gates

| Gate | Timing | Release condition |
|---|---|---|
| Gate 0 - Baseline | Before funding | Approved hypothesis, baseline, owner, investment and expected GP/PBO |
| Gate 1 - Pilot | Weeks 4-6 | Execution achieved and leading indicators respond |
| Gate 2 - Scale | Weeks 8-12 | Positive incremental GP, acceptable payback and no service-level breach |
| Gate 3 - Portfolio review | Monthly | Reallocate funding toward the highest risk-adjusted incremental PBO |

### Core KPIs

- Incremental Gross Profit and PBO
- ROI, payback and spend-to-incremental-TO
- Brand and channel TO growth
- Market value growth and share movement
- Discount/GSV and Gross Margin
- Supply Chain Cost/TO, OTIF, fill rate, OOS and write-offs
- Working capital and customer inventory where available

### Decision gates and guardrails

1. Protect a minimum PBO outcome at portfolio level; every exception must state the expected recovery and timing.
2. Require positive incremental Gross Profit before scale funding.
3. Do not use absolute supply-chain cost reduction as the productivity metric; assess cost rate and service together.
4. Do not scale SKY investment until internal Sales and Market measurement differences are reconciled.
5. Scale COBALT only where incremental PBO remains attractive after the required investment.

### Additional data required

- Initiative-level marketing spend and committed liabilities
- Incremental GP/PBO model with approved product/customer costs
- Weekly internal Sales, POS and inventory for SKY reconciliation
- Distribution, price and promotion by brand/channel
- Working-capital and service-level impact

## Interpretation and reporting guardrails

- Report Market MAT and internal FY Sales as separate periods.
- Describe MT as the relative-performance hotspot and DT as the larger absolute internal TO gap.
- Do not claim that holiday seasonality caused the weak months without weekly multi-year evidence.
- Do not describe the decline as customer attrition; active customers changed only from 19 to 18 and the observed exit was immaterial.
- Do not claim that higher discount caused the full TO decline or that lower discount alone will recover it.
- Do not claim improved supply-chain efficiency from lower absolute expenditure.
- Do not claim that lower marketing caused the market-share decline; the current data establishes co-movement, not causality.
- Treat SKY's market/internal divergence as a diagnostic question until period, sell-in/sell-out, inventory and distribution are reconciled.
